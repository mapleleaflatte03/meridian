## 2024-06-03 - Performance issue with copy.deepcopy()
**Learning:** Found multiple instances of `copy.deepcopy()` in the codebase. As noted in the memory, `copy.deepcopy()` is slow but replacing it with `json.loads(json.dumps())` or `dict()` to improve performance carries significant risks, and replacing them with `dict()` carries shared-state mutation risks for nested objects. Only apply these replacements if the objects strictly require only shallow copying or are 100% JSON-compatible without semantic shifts. However, in `institution_brain_policy.py`, `status_surface.py`, `workspace.py`, and `kernel/workspace.py`, `copy.deepcopy()` is used for objects that are strictly JSON-serializable configurations/payloads, and we can replace them to improve performance if we are careful and ensure no `datetime`, `UUID`, or non-serializable objects exist. Let's look closely at `status_surface.py` or `workspace.py`.

**Action:** Before optimizing `copy.deepcopy()`, rigorously check what types of objects are being copied. If they are just basic dicts with strings/ints, use `json.loads(json.dumps())` or a custom fast deepcopy function instead of Python's generic `copy.deepcopy()` for measurable performance gains on critical paths.
